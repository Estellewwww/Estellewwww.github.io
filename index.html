01 / MY WORK

From vehicle markets
to portfolio decisions.

At RVI Group, a residual value insurer, I build the models that estimate what a vehicle will be worth years from now, test them against what the market actually paid, and automate the pipelines that turn those forecasts into prices. The work covers nearly every make and model across the UK, Australia, and China.

01

Predictive modeling

I build and maintain regression, GLM, and gradient boosting (XGBoost) models that forecast residual values and feed directly into guaranteed future value (GFV) pricing.

XGBoost · GLM · Regression
02

Feature engineering & selection

I engineer features from vehicle age, mileage, MSRP, segment trends, and macroeconomic indicators, and use Lasso and SHAP to select features and explain what drives each forecast.

Lasso · SHAP · Interpretability
03

Validation & backtesting

I backtest forecasts out-of-time against realized auction values using MAPE and RMSE, and strengthen current market value (CMV) estimates with comparable vehicle tables and guidebook benchmarks such as CAP.

Out-of-time testing · MAPE / RMSE · CMV
04

Production pipelines

I automated our end-to-end workflow in Python and SQL, from data upload, cleaning, and QC through CMV and pricing calculations to enrollment and claims, cutting manual processing time by more than 80%.

Python · SQL · 80%+ less manual work
HOW THE WORK CONNECTS

A forecast travels through the whole policy.

Because I work on every stage, I see how a model's error turns into a price, a loss, and the next model revision.

  1. 01 / FORECAST

    Model future vehicle values

    Train and compare models on vehicle and market data, and check how values vary across age, term, and market.

  2. 02 / PRICE

    Turn uncertainty into a premium

    Combine forecasts, scenario analysis, and loss experience to set guaranteed future values and recommend pricing adjustments.

  3. 03 / ENROLL

    Track the book being written

    Automate enrollment processing so policy data is clean, checked, and ready for pricing and loss monitoring.

  4. 04 / CLAIM

    Close the loop at maturity

    Run quarterly loss model calculations and verify claims, then compare outcomes with the original forecast to improve the next one.

Open the methodology & limitations +

This is a deliberately simplified insurance demonstration, not an investment or insurance quote. Initial vehicle prices, segment mix and depreciation assumptions are invented. Each vehicle’s reference maturity value follows annual compound depreciation (compact ICE 14%, SUV ICE 12%, BEV 20%). The guarantee is 95% of that reference value.

Claim = max(guaranteed maturity value − simulated resale value, 0)

Market and BEV shocks shift reference resale values. Normally distributed errors combine a shared market factor and vehicle-specific noise (40% common-factor variance). Volatility is a percentage of reference maturity value. Resale values are floored at zero. We simulate 400 outcomes for 600 vehicles using a fixed seed. Expected claims are the sample mean; the 95th percentile is an empirical quantile of total claims. Expenses are assumed at 1% of initial portfolio value for the full term. No discounting, recoveries, mileage model, claim timing, adverse selection, parameter estimation or regulatory capital model is included. The finite simulations and assumed normal errors are limitations, especially for extreme tail risk. Vehicle segments are illustrative and not estimates of current markets.

Changing premium affects loss ratio and margin, but does not change vehicle values or expected claims. Changing BEV count share also changes total initial portfolio value because the synthetic segments have different prices.
